## In my own words (no peeking at the source while writing this)

Atoms have protons (+), electrons (-), and neutrons (no charge). Protons and
neutrons are stuck tight in the nucleus and don't move. Electrons are loosely
held and can jump between atoms. When you rub two materials together,
electrons physically move from one to the other — one material ends up with
extra electrons (negative), the other ends up short (positive). That
imbalance creates an attractive force between them. "Static" just means the
charge is sitting still instead of flowing.

The positive/negative labels are backwards from how Franklin originally
guessed — he thought the "fluid" flowed the opposite way — but the naming
stuck anyway. Electrons carry negative charge purely by historical accident,
not because of anything intrinsic to being negative.

## How this connects to something I already know

Not much prior electronics knowledge yet, but this is basically the same
idea as gravity/pressure differences causing things to move toward
equilibrium — a charge imbalance "wants" to even out, the same way water
flows downhill toward a lower level. That intuition should carry over
directly to voltage next.
